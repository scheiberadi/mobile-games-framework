# Map rework (pannable landscape with places, walking characters, settings)

Date: 2026-09-25. Status: approved by the user (design section by section, then eight review changes, then one final clarification about road waypoints, all folded in below). Part of Eva's Learning World (`EvasLearningWorld/`). This is step 1 of two: step 2 (side-view walking art for the child and Eva) is a separate spec, plan and phone test, and is not designed here.

## Goal

The current Map is three building icons in a row on a soft landscape, with Eva and the child's character standing large on the right. Replace it with a painted landscape that behaves like a small world: the house in the middle, other places spread around it with roads leading from the house to each, small characters playing near the house at first and later near the last place visited, and a walk along the road to a tapped place before it opens. The world must have room for the roughly eleven places the product plans (House, School, Store, Playground now; Arcade, Zoo and farm, Science lab, Workshop, Art studio, Brain gym, Friends' park later), which a single 1440 x 900 screen cannot hold at the 240-unit tap minimum.

## Decisions (from the design conversation)

- Layout: house in the centre, School and Store around it, roads radiating from the house. Not an island; an ordinary landscape.
- World size: a painted landscape about 2880 x 1350 units (twice the screen wide, 1.5 times tall), centre origin, house near the centre. The screen is a 1440 x 900 window on it.
- Places are added by new art plus one catalogue entry. Only places that exist are shown: no locks, no "coming soon".
- Characters are small (about 160 units tall). They walk to a tapped place along the roads and the place opens on arrival.
- Step 1 uses today's front-facing character rigs, moved along the road in small hops. This hop is explicitly temporary until step 2 provides real side-view walking art. Step 1 adds no walking animation states, blend logic or abstractions to make the prototype rigs look like proper walking.
- Art is generated by the user with ChatGPT from prompts Claude writes (house workflow). Claude draws only the gear icon.

## Initial composition (an explicit design decision)

World units, centre origin (x right, y up), the house is the hub. The first view (the map's initial camera) is centred on (0, 0), so it shows x -720..720, y -450..450. Tap boxes (the building's tappable rectangle, centre and size):

| Element | Centre (x, y) | Size | Rectangle x | Rectangle y |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| House | (60, -30) | 320 x 280 | -100..220 | -170..110 |
| School | (-400, 40) | 280 x 240 | -540..-260 | -80..160 |
| Store | (470, -250) | 280 x 240 | 330..610 | -370..-130 |
| Characters at the House (start spot, tap area) | (-120, -300) | 240 x 240 | -240..0 | -420..-180 |
| Characters at the School (tap area) | (-400, -210) | 240 x 240 | -520..-280 | -330..-90 |
| Characters at the Store (tap area) | (200, -320) | 240 x 240 | 80..320 | -440..-200 |

Checked constraints (a Rules test asserts all of them, so the composition cannot drift silently):

- Every building tap box is at least 240 x 240 and lies inside the first view with at least 60 units of margin from the view edges. The characters' areas are at least 240 x 240 and lie inside the first view.
- No two building tap boxes overlap; gaps are at least 100 units between buildings (House to School 160, House to Store 110, School to Store 590 horizontally). A characters' area never overlaps any building tap box (so waving Eva never steals a building tap); the smallest gap is 10 units (School area to the School box).
- School and Store lie clear of the top-left settings button zone (x -690..-450, y 175..415) and the coin counter zone (x 450..690, y 340..430).
- School upper-left and Store lower-right of the House form the triangle the user chose; the settings gear top-left and the coin counter top-right are the only screen-fixed elements.

These numbers are the design values. If the generated art forces a change, the change is made here in the spec first, then in `Places`, and the composition test is updated with it.

## Data (Rules layer, pure C#)

`Places` (new, `Rules/Places.cs`): `enum PlaceId { House, School, Store }` and `sealed class Place` with `Id`, `ScreenKey` (name of the app-layer `ScreenId`, a string so Rules stays engine-free), `TapBox` (centre and size, the table above), `Road` (explicit waypoints from the shared junction outward to the place's standing spot, world units; the House's road is the junction alone), `StandingSpot` (where the characters stand at the place, the centre of their tap area), `RoadBox` (where the road picture is placed, containing every waypoint), `BuildingSprite`, `RoadSprite` (none for the House), `VoiceKey`. `Places.All` returns the declared, fixed order. Playground is added when its games exist.

The explicit waypoint data in `Places` is the authoritative gameplay representation; the road art is drawn to follow it, never the other way round. There is no image-tracing step. To make the art follow the waypoints, a tiny script draws a layout guide (the road centrelines and building boxes on the world backdrop frame) from the same data, which the user attaches to ChatGPT as the reference for the road pictures. The phone review verifies that the characters visibly stay on the painted paths; if a painted road strays, the road is regenerated or the waypoints are nudged in `Places`, not the other way round with code that reads pixels. A small test also checks that the layout data file the art scripts read matches `Places`, so the two cannot drift.

`MapPath` (new, pure): `Route(from, to)` returns the waypoint list between two places, always through the house junction (a trip School to Store walks the School road backwards to the house, then the Store road). `Duration(route)` returns the trip time: constant speed, capped at 2.5 seconds (long trips walk faster). `PositionAt(route, t)` returns the point along the route for a normalised time, used by the screen.

`MapCamera` (new, pure): clamps a requested view centre so the 1440 x 900 window stays inside the world, and `CentreOn(point)` returns the clamped centre.

## Screen (App layer)

`MapScreen` is rebuilt (same `ScreenId.Map`):

- Layers from back to front: landscape backdrop (one picture; if it exceeds the texture size limit it is cut into two halves), one road picture per place, one building picture per place, then the two characters. The building picture itself is the tap target: no oversized or invisible hit areas beyond the picture's own silhouette box in the table above.
- The world sits under a content transform; dragging moves it (clamped by `MapCamera`). A drag that starts on a building is still a tap if the pointer barely moved.
- On show: the view centres on the characters' standing spot (see below).
- Tap on a building, deterministic and short: the place's Eva voice line starts and the characters begin moving in the same call, the view follows them, dragging and further taps are locked, and on arrival `Navigator.Show(place screen)`. The voice line is never awaited. If the characters are already at that place the screen opens at once. The trip takes at most 2.5 seconds.
- Characters: first launch they stand beside the house (start spot above); afterwards beside the last place visited (`Progress.LastPlace`, saved on arrival). They keep the existing tap-to-wave and idle behaviour while standing. While walking they hop along the route (Eva bounces; the child bobs gently), a temporary prototype until step 2.
- The global Home button stays hidden on the Map (existing behaviour); other screens' Home returns to the Map, so the child sees the characters where they left them.
- Top-left gear button (small icon, tap area still 240 units): opens the parent gate.

## Tutorial

The tutorial hand keeps pointing at the right building (`GoToSchool`: School; `GoToStore`: Store; House stays as today). School and Store lie inside the first view (composition above), so the hand never needs scrolling. Hand positions are computed from world positions through the content transform.

## Settings (adult-facing)

Child-facing gameplay stays reading-free (the no-reading audit is unchanged for it). The parent gate and the settings panel are adult-facing, so they may use normal readable text; the no-reading audit exempts these two screens by name (their tap-target and overlap audits still apply).

- `ParentGateScreen` (new): one adult-knowledge question as normal text, with multiple-choice answers, randomised from a pool of about 20 culturally neutral questions and randomised answer order (per the parent spec). A correct answer opens the settings panel; a wrong answer or leaving returns to the Map. It is an accidental-access barrier, not security.
- `SettingsScreen` (new), kept simple: music on/off, voice volume, and "start over" with a second confirmation. Voice volume is three large discrete choices (low, medium, high); the existing audio system has no slider. The app has no background music today, so the music switch is dropped from this step rather than building music machinery for it (the plan records this). Settings therefore hold voice volume and "start over".

## Art (produced by the user with ChatGPT; Claude writes the prompts in the plan)

- Roadless landscape backdrop, about 2880 x 1350, same style as the house art, clear ground where the places sit and where future places will appear.
- One building picture each for House, School and Store, isolated on a clean background.
- One road picture per place.
- Prefer true alpha (transparent background) pictures for roads and buildings, and cut them out with the import script when generation gives a flat background, rather than shipping rectangles. On the phone the composition must show no seams, rectangles, mismatched ground colours or compositing edges.
- Roads must look like natural paths through the landscape (worn earth, gravel or cobbles that curve, with soft edges that blend into the grass), never like UI connector lines, dotted lines or arrows.
- Placeholders (generated by a small script) are used to build and test; the phone check waits for the real art. The backdrop is imported as two 1440 x 1350 halves to stay inside the texture size limit.

## Testing

Rules (edit mode, engine-free): `Places` (non-empty, ids unique, fixed order, every place has screen, sprites, road with at least two waypoints); the composition constraints above (tap sizes, margins inside the first view, no overlaps, minimum gaps, clear of the gear and coin zones); `MapPath` (route between every pair goes through the house, duration capped at 2.5 seconds and shorter for short trips, `PositionAt` endpoints); `MapCamera` (clamping at every edge, centring near an edge).

App (edit mode): buildings at least 240 and non-overlapping; tap starts the voice and the walk together and opens the screen only after arrival (advance the walk in the test); a second tap during the walk is ignored; the last place is saved and restored and the characters stand there on show; the tutorial hand targets computed from world positions; parent gate accepts only the right answer and returns to the Map otherwise; settings controls change their saved values; the audits include the new screens (with the two adult-facing screens exempt from the no-reading rule only). The existing suite keeps passing (Map tests updated for the rebuilt screen).

Phone review is a product gate for the composition, not only a camera test. It must confirm:

- The House clearly reads as the central hub.
- School and Store are immediately visible in the first view and distinguishable from each other and from the House.
- The roads lead naturally from the House to each destination and look like paths in the landscape, not connector lines.
- The world feels like a small place, not a background with icons placed on it.
- The three building tap targets are visually obvious without oversized or invisible hit areas.
- No seams, rectangles, ground colour mismatches or compositing edges show anywhere in the composition.
- Dragging is comfortable, the walk is short (at most 2.5 seconds) and the view follows it smoothly, and the gear is discreet.

## Out of scope

Side-view walking art and any walking animation states or abstractions (step 2), the Playground games, the other planned places, locks or progress on places, changing other screens, a free-scrolling world beyond the fixed landscape, a full settings system beyond the three controls.
